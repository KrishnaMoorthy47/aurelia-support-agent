# UAE Retail CFD Protections

## Why retail CFD rules are strict in the UAE
UAE regulators treat leveraged CFDs and rolling spot forex as high-risk products for retail clients. In the Dubai International Financial Centre (DIFC), the Dubai Financial Services Authority (DFSA) calls them Restricted Speculative Investments and sets specific rules for them in its Conduct of Business module (COB section 6.16). In Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM), the Financial Services Regulatory Authority (FSRA) has its own rules for over-the-counter leveraged products, covering margin close-out, referrals by unregulated third parties and the use of credit cards and credit facilities. Aurelia is a fictional onshore broker supervised by the Capital Market Authority (CMA) in this demo, and it applies the same retail protections described below.

## Margin limits for retail clients
Under the DFSA rules, a retail client must post a margin of at least 3.3% of the position's value for major currency pairs, 5% for non-major currency pairs, major equity indices, government bonds and gold, 10% for other equity indices and commodities other than gold, 50% for crypto tokens, and 20% for anything else. Aurelia's retail leverage matches these limits: 1:30 on major forex pairs, 1:20 on minor and exotic pairs, gold and major indices, 1:10 on other commodities and 1:2 on cryptocurrency CFDs.

## Automatic close-out at 50%
The DFSA rules require a firm to close all of a retail client's open positions, as soon as market conditions allow, when the client's net equity falls below 50% of the margin deposited in the account. Aurelia's stop-out is set at the same 50% level: when the margin level falls to 50%, the platform closes positions automatically. This limits how much of the deposited margin a client can lose in a fast market.

## You cannot lose more than your account balance
Retail clients have negative balance protection: their losses on CFD trading are limited to the funds in their trading account, so the broker cannot ask them to pay more. If a market gap takes an Aurelia retail account below zero, Aurelia resets the balance to zero. Professional clients do not have this protection.

## No deposit bonuses or trading incentives
DFSA rules prohibit firms from offering retail clients any incentive that is likely to encourage them to trade CFDs, including bonus offers, gifts, volume-based fee rebates, trading credits and rewards for opening an account. Aurelia does not offer deposit bonuses, trading credits, cashback or referral rewards to retail clients. If you see an advert promising an Aurelia bonus, treat it as a possible scam and report it to support.

## Credit cards cannot be used to fund trading
DFSA rules require firms to take reasonable steps so that retail clients do not pay margin with a credit card or any other borrowed money; debit cards are allowed. Aurelia therefore accepts debit cards but not credit cards for deposits by UAE retail clients, and asks every retail client to confirm that they are not trading with borrowed money. A card deposit made with a credit card is declined automatically.

## Introducers and referrals
DFSA rules prohibit firms from accepting a paid referral of a retail client from a person who is not regulated, and ADGM's rules also cover referrals by unregulated third parties. Aurelia only pays referral fees to regulated firms. No Aurelia employee or partner will ask you to trade through a personal "account manager" on social media.

## The risk warning you receive before trading
Before opening a CFD account for a retail client, DFSA rules require a written risk warning in a separate document, not hidden in the client agreement. It must explain that CFDs are complex, that there is a high probability of losing money rapidly because of leverage, and the percentage of retail accounts that lost money over the last 12 months, updated at least every quarter. Aurelia sends its risk warning by email before the account opens. Its current figure is that 71% of retail client accounts lost money when trading CFDs over the last 12 months (a demo value), shown on its website and platform.

## Appropriateness check
DFSA rules say a firm may only let a retail client trade CFDs after assessing whether the client has enough knowledge and experience to understand the risks and can absorb significant losses. Aurelia runs this check with a short questionnaire before the first trade; if CFDs do not look appropriate, Aurelia explains the risks and offers the demo account instead.
