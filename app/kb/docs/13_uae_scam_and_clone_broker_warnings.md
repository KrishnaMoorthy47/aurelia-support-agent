# UAE Scam and Clone Broker Warnings

## How to check that a broker is real
Before sending money to any broker, look it up yourself on the regulator's public register: the Capital Market Authority (CMA) list of licensed companies for onshore UAE firms, the DFSA public register for firms in the DIFC, and the FSRA public register for firms in ADGM. Compare the company name, address and website on the register with the ones you are dealing with. A licence number shown in an advert, a social media profile or a chat message is not proof, because scammers copy real numbers.

## Clone websites and fake apps
A clone firm copies the name, logo and licence details of a real broker on a website or app with a slightly different address. Only use the Aurelia website and the platform apps linked from it, and check the web address before you log in. Aurelia's real domain is aurelia-markets.example in this demo. If you find a site or app that looks like Aurelia but uses a different address, do not log in or deposit, and report it to support.

## Fake account managers on WhatsApp and social media
Scammers often contact people on WhatsApp, Telegram, Instagram or dating apps, pretend to be an "account manager" or "senior analyst" from a known broker, and promise guaranteed profits or signals. Aurelia staff never contact clients first on social media, never promise profits, never manage trades for you and never ask for your password or one-time codes.

## Requests to pay fees or send money
The CMA warns that it never asks investors to pay fees or dues through social media. Aurelia never asks you to send money to a personal bank account, a crypto wallet or a third party, and never asks for a "tax", "release fee" or "verification fee" before you can withdraw. Deposits to Aurelia only go to the payment methods shown inside your own client area.

## Calls pretending to be a government authority
The UAE Federal Authority for Identity, Citizenship, Customs and Ports Security (ICP) warns that fraudsters use its name and logo in fake calls and counterfeit websites to steal personal data. Calls that claim to come from a regulator, the police or a bank and ask you to share your Emirates ID details, card numbers or one-time codes should be ended; call the organisation back on the number listed on its official website.

## What to do if you think you were targeted
Stop all payments and do not share any more personal information. Tell Aurelia support straight away so a ticket can be logged and the security team can block access to your account if needed. You can also report unlicensed firms to the CMA, and report fraud to the police through the official channels. Aurelia support cannot recover money sent to a third party, but it can tell you whether a payment request really came from Aurelia.
